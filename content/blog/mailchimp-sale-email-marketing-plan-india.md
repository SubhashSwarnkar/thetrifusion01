Intuit has agreed to sell Mailchimp to Bending Spoons for about $3.68 billion, with closing expected in early 2027. Nothing breaks today, but Indian businesses that depend on Mailchimp should use the next few months to export their data, fix sender authentication, review consent records and decide whether to stay, switch or own more of their marketing stack.

This guide is written for founders, marketing heads and owners of small and mid-sized businesses in Jaipur, across Rajasthan and the rest of India who send newsletters, offers, order updates or lead nurture emails. It is not a "panic and migrate" piece. It is a calm checklist so a change of owner at your email tool does not become a change in your revenue.

## What actually happened

On 19 November 2026, Intuit announced in its first-quarter results that it had signed a definitive agreement to sell Mailchimp to Bending Spoons for approximately $3.68 billion: $3.31 billion in cash and $368 million in newly issued Bending Spoons shares ([Intuit press release](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1331/intuit-reports-first-quarter-results-and-enters-agreement-to-sell-mailchimp-to-bending-spoons)). Bending Spoons, based in Milan, confirmed the deal the same day and said it expects to close in early 2027, subject to regulatory approvals ([Bending Spoons announcement](https://investors.bendingspoons.com/newsroom/bending-spoons-to-acquire-mailchimp)).

[TechCrunch's report](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/bending-spoons-buys-mailchimp-for-3-68-billion/) puts the deal in context: Intuit bought Mailchimp in 2021 for roughly $12 billion, so this is a large markdown. TechCrunch also notes that Bending Spoons has become a buyer of mature web products, picking up AOL, Eventbrite, Airtable and Miro in the last year, after earlier deals for Evernote, Meetup and Vimeo, and that it typically moves products onto centralised engineering and revenue systems.

Bending Spoons' CEO Luca Ferrari said the company will "invest substantially in the business" after closing. Intuit said it is focused on a smooth transition for customers. Those are the official positions, and they matter.

## Why Indian Mailchimp users should pay attention

A change of owner is not automatically bad. But the buyer's track record is public, and it is reasonable to plan around it.

- **Pricing has moved after past deals.** After Bending Spoons bought Evernote in 2023, the annual price of the app rose from $100 to $249, according to [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/bending-spoons-nasdaq-debut-european-startup-success-1806672), which also reports that Vimeo and WeTransfer users saw similar increases.
- **Teams have shrunk.** TechCrunch reported in 2024 that Bending Spoons planned to lay off about 75% of WeTransfer staff after that acquisition ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/08/bending-spoons-plans-to-lay-off-75-of-wetransfer-staff-after-acquisition/)), and Reuters reported staff cuts and product changes at Eventbrite in April 2026 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/bending-spoons-cuts-eventbrite-staff-rolls-out-product-changes-after-takeover-2026-04-13/)).
- **Plans and features get rationalised.** When a product is moved onto a new owner's billing and engineering systems, free tiers, legacy plans, integrations and support channels are the things most often reviewed.

None of this tells us what will happen to Mailchimp specifically. It does tell an Indian business owner that the next 6 to 12 months are a sensible window to reduce dependency on any single marketing tool, whoever owns it.

For Indian SMEs the stakes are slightly different from those of a US company. Most Mailchimp plans are billed in US dollars, so a price change also comes with currency exposure. Many Indian teams also run email alongside WhatsApp, SMS and phone follow-up, which means the email tool is often wired into a CRM, a website form, a billing system or a Shopify or WooCommerce store. A change in pricing or API access ripples through all of those connections.

## The risk is not the tool, it is where your data lives

The single biggest lesson from any SaaS acquisition is this: **your customer list, consent history and engagement data are business assets, and they should never exist in only one vendor's database.**

Ask yourself three questions:

1. If my email tool doubled its price next month, could I move in a week?
2. Do I have a copy of every subscriber, with the date and source of their consent, outside the tool?
3. Do my website forms, store and CRM send data into the email tool directly, or through something I control?

If the honest answer to any of these is "no", the Mailchimp sale is a useful nudge to fix it, whatever you decide about Mailchimp itself.

## Step-by-step: what to do in the next 30 days

### Step 1: Export everything, today

Mailchimp lets you export an entire audience as a CSV from the Audience section, and it emails you when the file is ready ([Mailchimp help: view or export contacts](https://mailchimp.com/help/view-export-contacts/)). It also offers a full account data export for backup purposes ([Mailchimp help: export and back up account data](https://mailchimp.com/help/export-back-up-data/)).

Export at least:

- All audiences, including unsubscribed and cleaned contacts (you need these to keep honouring opt-outs on any future platform).
- Tags, groups and merge fields.
- Campaign reports for the last 12 months, so you have baseline open, click and unsubscribe rates.
- Signup form settings and the exact consent text shown to subscribers.

Store the files in a secure, access-controlled location, not in someone's personal Downloads folder. Customer lists are personal data.

### Step 2: Map every integration

Make a simple sheet of everything that talks to your email tool:

- Website and landing-page forms
- E-commerce store (order and abandoned-cart flows)
- CRM or lead management system
- Payment or billing software
- Zapier, Make or custom webhook scripts
- WhatsApp or SMS tools that share the same contact list

For each, note who owns it, how it connects (native plugin, API key, webhook) and what would break if the email tool changed. This sheet is what turns a scary migration into a two-week project.

### Step 3: Fix sender authentication on your own domain

Whatever tool you use, inbox placement depends heavily on your domain setup. Google's sender guidelines require every sender to Gmail to set up SPF or DKIM, keep spam rates reported in Postmaster Tools below 0.3%, and use TLS. Senders of more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail accounts must also set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and marketing messages need one-click unsubscribe ([Google, Email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126?hl=en)).

This matters for a migration because authentication records are tied to your domain, not to Mailchimp. If you set them up properly, with a sending subdomain such as `news.yourbrand.in`, you can move providers later by updating DNS records instead of rebuilding your reputation from zero.

### Step 4: Audit consent before India's DPDP deadlines

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 are being phased in. Rules 1, 2 and 17 to 21 came into force on publication in November 2025, Rule 4 (consent managers) one year later, and Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 eighteen months after publication, which most commentators place at 13 May 2027 ([DPDP Rules, Rule 1](https://dpdpr.in/rules/rule-1/)).

Rule 3 sets out what a notice to a data principal must contain: an itemised description of the personal data, the specific purpose, and a way to withdraw consent that is as easy as giving it ([DPDP Rules, Rule 3](https://dpdpr.in/rules/rule-3/)). For email marketing this means you should be able to show, for each subscriber, what they signed up for, when, and how they can leave.

Messaging by SMS and calls is separately governed by TRAI's commercial communication regulations (TCCCPR), which sit alongside the DPDP framework ([Legal 500 overview](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/marketing-consent-in-india-navigating-trais-tcccpr-and-the-dpdp-act)). If you plan to combine email, SMS and WhatsApp in one platform, consent should be captured per channel.

This is not legal advice, and your legal counsel should confirm what applies to your business. But a platform change is the cheapest moment to clean consent data, because you are touching every record anyway.

### Step 5: Decide: stay, switch, or own the core

Only after the first four steps should you decide on the tool. You now have data, a map and a baseline, so the decision is about fit, not fear.

## Stay, switch or own: a comparison

| Option | Best for | Main benefits | Main risks | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Stay on Mailchimp and monitor** | Teams happy with the product, small lists, few integrations | No migration work; deal is not closed until early 2027 | Future plan, price or support changes are outside your control; USD billing | Low |
| **Switch to another SaaS email tool** | Teams that want INR billing, bundled WhatsApp/SMS, or a tool already in their stack | Can reduce cost and consolidate channels | Same vendor-dependency problem with a new name; migration and warm-up time | Medium |
| **Own the core: CRM + sending service** | Businesses with sales teams, repeat customers or custom workflows | Customer data, segments and automations live in your own system; email provider becomes swappable | Needs build and maintenance; deliverability must be managed carefully | Medium to high |
| **Hybrid: CRM as source of truth, SaaS tool for campaigns** | Most growing Indian SMEs | Easy campaign design plus a clean, portable customer database | Requires a reliable sync between CRM and email tool | Medium |

For many businesses we speak to in Jaipur and across Rajasthan, the hybrid route is the practical middle ground. The CRM holds the customer, the deal history and the consent record. The email tool, whichever one it is, receives a synced segment and sends the campaign. If the tool changes, the CRM does not.

## How to evaluate a Mailchimp alternative in India

There are many lists ranking email tools, and prices change often, so we will not repeat a price table that will be stale next quarter. Instead, use these criteria when you shortlist any platform:

### Commercial checks

- **Billing currency and GST invoice.** Does the vendor bill in INR and issue GST-compliant invoices? This affects both cost predictability and input tax credit.
- **What counts as a "contact".** Some tools bill for unsubscribed or inactive contacts; others bill on emails sent. Model your real list, not the headline price.
- **Contract terms.** Look for the right to export data at any time, and notice periods for price changes.

### Technical checks

- **Custom domain authentication** (SPF, DKIM, DMARC alignment) and one-click unsubscribe support.
- **API and webhooks** so your website, store and CRM can sync without fragile workarounds.
- **Data export** of contacts, tags, events and consent fields in standard formats.
- **Data location and processing terms**, which you will need for DPDP readiness.

### Marketing checks

- **Multi-channel support** if your customers respond better on WhatsApp than on email, which is common in India.
- **Automation depth**: welcome series, abandoned cart, re-engagement, lead scoring.
- **Template editor and mobile rendering**, since a large share of Indian users read email on phones.

### Support checks

- Support hours that overlap with Indian working hours.
- A clear migration path (import tools, list-hygiene guidance, IP or domain warm-up advice).

## A migration checklist you can actually follow

If you do decide to move, here is the sequence that protects deliverability and avoids losing subscribers:

- [ ] Full export from the old tool (contacts, unsubscribes, bounces, tags, reports)
- [ ] Suppression list imported first, so you never email someone who opted out
- [ ] Remove hard bounces and contacts with no engagement for 12+ months, or run a re-confirmation campaign before moving them
- [ ] SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up on a sending subdomain and verified
- [ ] One-click unsubscribe header and a visible unsubscribe link in every marketing email
- [ ] Website and landing-page forms switched to the new tool, with the consent text preserved
- [ ] Store and CRM integrations reconnected and tested with test contacts
- [ ] Warm-up plan: start with your most engaged segment and grow volume over two to four weeks
- [ ] Postmaster Tools (Google) monitoring set up for spam rate and domain reputation
- [ ] Old account kept active, but not sending, until the new setup has run cleanly for a month
- [ ] Old account data deleted or archived according to your retention policy

## Why your website and CRM matter more than your email tool

Email platforms are only as good as the data that flows into them. In practice, most deliverability and conversion problems we see in Indian SMEs start upstream:

- **Forms that do not capture consent properly**, such as a pre-ticked checkbox or no record of what the person agreed to.
- **No lead source tracking**, so the team cannot tell whether a subscriber came from Google, Instagram, a trade fair or a WhatsApp chat.
- **Duplicate and dirty records** across the website, the store and a sales spreadsheet.
- **No link between email activity and sales**, so marketing cannot prove which campaigns produced revenue.

Fixing these is usually a mix of three pieces of work: a cleaner [website with properly built lead forms](https://thetrifusion.in/services/website-development), a [custom CRM that acts as your single customer record](https://thetrifusion.in/services/crm-erp-development), and a [digital marketing plan](https://thetrifusion.in/services/digital-marketing) that uses that data well across email, search and social.

The principle is simple: the business should own its customer data, and tools such as email platforms should plug into it. That way a change of vendor, price or owner means reconnecting a tool, not rebuilding your customer base.

## A practical email plan for 2027, whichever tool you choose

Once your data and setup are under control, the strategy itself does not need to be complicated.

### 1. Segment by intent, not just by list

Split subscribers by what they did: enquired, bought once, bought repeatedly, downloaded a guide, attended an event. A Jaipur interior design firm, for instance, should not send the same email to someone who asked for a quote last week and someone who signed up for a newsletter two years ago.

### 2. Build three core automations first

- **Welcome series** (3 to 4 emails) that explains who you are and what to do next.
- **Lead nurture** for enquiries that have not converted, connected to your sales team's follow-up.
- **Re-engagement** for contacts who have stopped opening, ending with a clear option to unsubscribe.

These three usually do more for revenue than a weekly broadcast.

### 3. Coordinate email with WhatsApp, do not duplicate it

Many Indian customers respond faster on WhatsApp. Use email for detail (proposals, guides, invoices, long-form updates) and WhatsApp for short, time-sensitive nudges, with separate consent for each. Sending the same message on both channels on the same day tends to increase opt-outs.

### 4. Measure what matters

Opens are less reliable than they used to be because of privacy features in mail apps. Track clicks, replies, enquiries, and revenue per campaign. Watch spam complaints and unsubscribes closely, because Google's 0.3% spam-rate threshold is a hard ceiling, not a target ([Google sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126?hl=en)).

### 5. Review the stack every six months

Put a recurring review on the calendar: cost per month, cost per 1,000 emails, deliverability, and whether the tool's roadmap still fits. Acquisitions like this one are a reminder that the software market moves faster than most annual plans.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Migrating in a hurry.** The deal is not expected to close until early 2027. A rushed move with a cold domain can hurt inbox placement more than any price change.
- **Leaving unsubscribes behind.** If you only export active subscribers, you risk emailing people who opted out, which damages trust and may raise compliance issues.
- **Buying a list to "restart" on a new tool.** Purchased lists are a fast route to spam complaints and blocked domains, and they conflict with consent-based rules.
- **Letting one person hold all the logins.** Make sure the account owner is a company email address, with two-factor authentication and at least two admins.
- **Ignoring the CRM.** Switching email tools without fixing where customer data lives just moves the problem.
